Playing for a Fairer Future

One evaluation plan across three live regions — Belfast, Tower Hamlets and Doncaster — sharing one Imagination Cycle (Listen › Ask, Create › Build, Decide › Choose, Act › Do, Share › Show), adapted to each place, partner and age group.

West Belfast

Delivered with Blackie River Community Groups, led locally by Siobhan. Field notes below are from the first weeks of sessions and a family drop-in day.

Older kids & repeat engagement

Observation
  • A group of older kids were seen playing for playing's sake — no task needed to keep them engaged.
  • One boy came back and made another robot — a repeat visitor, excited to build on what he'd made before and added more to it.
  • Still working out how to understand and reach engagement among older kids, including through schools.

Parents & carers

Observation

A dad sat through the whole session with his child, and was interested enough afterwards to leave his work phone number — an opening for further involvement.

Stories of change

A mum on the Saturday session started out risk-averse and nervous, playing with the scissors — later that day she ended up upstairs confidently making a ship on her own. She has twins with autism and said she couldn't bring everyone together, but that the session had opened her mind to how she might introduce this kind of play at home. Several parents said they were “trying this at home.”

Widening thinking with the spoke

Observation

Using the spoke got children finding metal in different places — widening how they think about materials. One child who'd been restless beforehand became fascinated and engrossed once engaged with it.

Delivery adjustment

Session hours were changed to run an afternoon slot specifically for training on how to use the spoke.

Materials in progress

In development

A booklet of play ideas is being put together, including iOi's own material — junk art among it.

What's next

Planning
  • A summer scheme is being considered for next summer, running over a couple of weeks for 0–12 year olds.
  • Programme planning and dates are being set from September onwards.
  • Reaching different ages and reaching out to different groups.

Completed project · Impact Report 2026

Building Brighter Futures

Funded by the Aldgate & Allhallows Foundation. Building Brighter Futures set out to foster stronger family bonds and develop participants' creative skills through shared, hands-on design challenges that build children's cognitive, social and emotional skills — delivered with English Martyrs and Young V&A.

8student & caregiver co-design sessions
2immersive family workshops
455participants — children, parents/carers and educators
274Imagination Boxes distributed

What we did

Three strands of activity, each designed to be co-created with the families taking part.

  • Ran eight co-design sessions with students and caregivers using iOi activities to capture their insights on play.
  • Designed two STEAM-based immersive family events to encourage collaboration, problem-solving and play for families.
  • Shared Imagination Boxes with participants to encourage play at home.

Reach & engagement

344participants across co-design sessions
111attendees at immersive family events
253%increase in views of 'iOi at home' resources after delivery
+89Net Promoter Score, caregiver engagement & satisfaction

Community context

DfE data: participating schools compared to the English national average.

85%more pupils with SEN EHCPs
14%more pupils receiving SEN support
246%more pupils whose first language isn't English
53%more pupils who had been eligible for free school meals in the last 6 years

Creative thinking & holistic skills

15%increase in children's creative thinking (DAT measurement)
35%average increase across holistic skill development

Children were more likely to report increased confidence in (LEGO Children's Tool measurement): thinking of different ideas (43%), solving problems (69%), building things (54%), exploring new things (35%), communicating ideas to others (32%), using things they've learnt to help them (11%), trying even if things are hard (4%).

Our outcomes

  • Increase creative thinking skills
  • Increase holistic skills development
  • Increase levels of caregiver satisfaction & advocacy

What we learned

Creative thinking & holistic skills

Creative learning acted as a vehicle for wider skills development. Alongside the 15% increase in creative thinking scores, LEGO Children's Tool results showed growth across problem-solving, communication, exploration and confidence in making and building. Facilitator observations further highlighted increased experimentation, creative risk-taking and perseverance.

High engagement and positive outcomes show the co-design approach successfully delivered inclusive and accessible opportunities for diverse families.

Family relationships

Children consistently expressed a desire for more opportunities to play and create with parents and carers. Findings suggest the value of creative family experiences lies less in the activity itself and more in creating dedicated time and space for families to collaborate, communicate and enjoy shared experiences together.

Caregiver confidence

Caregiver confidence, rather than interest, was often the primary barrier to creative activity at home. Parents and carers frequently spoke positively about creativity and recognised its importance, but mentioned barriers including time, mess, uncertainty and lack of ideas. The combination of co-design, family participation and take-home resources appeared effective in addressing these barriers and increasing confidence.

Co-design sessions

Four sessions with students and educators, prompted to consider what play is to them, what they play with, and to create "play manifestos" — dream playhouses, storytelling, 3D cardboard prototyping and ScribbleBots. English Martyrs students co-designed a poster from the sessions.
